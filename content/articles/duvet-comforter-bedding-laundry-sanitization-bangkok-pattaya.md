## Why Domestic Condo Washers Fail on King/Queen Duvets & Mattress Toppers

Most condominium units in **Sukhumvit, Sathorn, Rama 9, Jomtien, and Wongamat** come equipped with compact 7 KG to 9 KG front-loading washing machines. While rated for "8 KG of dry laundry," drum volume—not motor weight—is what determines whether a bulky item can be cleaned properly.

### 1. Zero Water Circulation & Detergent Trapping
When a dry King-size comforter or quilted mattress protector expands with water, it fills 100% of a domestic washer drum. Because there is no free space for mechanical tumbling, water and detergent cannot flush through the dense inner batting. The outer shell gets wet while sweat, body oils, and soap scum remain trapped in the center.

### 2. Incomplete Spin Extraction & Bearing Damage
A waterlogged King duvet can weigh over **18 to 22 kilograms**. Domestic washing machines frequently abort their high-speed spin cycle due to drum imbalance—leaving the core of your comforter soaking wet and straining the machine's suspension bearings.

### 3. Tropical Mold, Mildew & Balcony Smog
Even if a condo washer finishes its cycle, drying a thick comforter or 600-thread-count bed sheet set indoors or on a compact balcony takes 24 to 48 hours in Thailand’s 70%–85% relative humidity. Within just 6 hours of trapped dampness inside microfiber or down-alternative batting, *Aspergillus* mold spores and odor-causing bacteria multiply rapidly, triggering morning nasal congestion, asthma, and skin irritation.

### 4. Dust Mites (*Dermatophagoides*) Survive Cold Condo Washes
Medical entomology research shows that tropical house dust mites thrive in warm, humid mattresses, pillowcases, and duvets. Cold or lukewarm 30°C domestic wash cycles do not eliminate live dust mites or denature their allergenic proteins (*Der p 1*).
